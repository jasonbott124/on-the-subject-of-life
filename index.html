    02About the show

    What is On the Subject of Life*?

    A growing digital archive of very human conversations about interesting things: language, history, geology, earth science, conservation, the questions that matter right now, and sometimes just life itself.

    Guests come from everywhere. When a conversation turns to research, the person across the table is an expert in it, but the show isn't only for experts. I'd be just as happy talking with a hospital janitor as with a professor. What ties it together is curiosity, and each conversation builds on the ones before it.

    Interesting never means loose with the facts. Every paper, study, and figure mentioned on the show is cited in the episode notes, and if something said turns out to be untrue, I go back and correct it or cut it.

    This podcast is my way of sharing the conversations I have about life on this planet, and of keeping an archive of the people and topics I find fascinating, however mainstream or niche.

    04About Jason

    Hi, I'm Jason, a somewhat well-traveled geophysicist (B.S., UT Austin) and research associate at the University of Texas Institute for Geophysics. I study how ice sheets and glaciers evolve, especially where they meet subglacial volcanism, using geophysical data, numerical modeling (including Stokes flow), and more recently machine learning.

    I'm also drawn to ecology: how Earth systems shape the habitats of the organisms living in them, and how those organisms shape Earth systems in turn. I care a great deal about wildlife, especially endangered species.
